# Example Profiles — What PsychoPrompt Generates

These are examples of the profiles the AI generates after the 7-question intake. Each reflects a different psychological pattern.

---

## Example 1 — The Paralyzed Visionary

*Typical intake answers: Started 12 projects, finished 0. Invisible wall = "I freeze before the first step." Worst case = "People see I'm not as smart as I seem." Motivation = internal but feels stuck. Gap from desired life = 9/10.*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: The Paralyzed Visionary
**Secondary Pattern**: Fear-of-Judgment Avoider

**Your Core Blocker**: The gap between where you are and where you want to be feels so vast that each small step seems meaningless — so you don't take it.

**What drives you**: Autonomy, mastery, and the idea of building something that proves your actual potential. Not money itself — but what money represents: freedom from external control.

**Your strengths**: You generate ideas that are genuinely valuable. Your pattern-recognition is strong. You understand problems at a deep level. You are capable of intense focus when you feel safe.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will always connect each small task to your larger vision — so nothing feels pointless
- I will give you one task at a time, never a list
- I will NOT push you to "just do it" when you're frozen — I will ask what makes this feel unsafe
- When you're stuck, I will help you find the smallest version of the action that feels manageable

**One thing to remember**: You don't lack intelligence. You lack evidence that starting small leads somewhere. We are building that evidence now, one step at a time.

## Example 2 — The External Executor

*Typical intake answers: Started several projects, none finished. Invisible wall = "I know I should but my body refuses." Worst case = "It doesn't matter, nothing matters." Motivation = external pressure (family, financial). Gap = 8/10.*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: The External Executor
**Secondary Pattern**: The Paralyzed Visionary

**Your Core Blocker**: You are running someone else's race. The brain does not sustainably perform for external rewards alone — especially when the task conflicts with your actual desires.

**What drives you**: You have genuine interests and ambitions underneath the external pressure. The work you'd actually choose is different from the work you're doing. Finding that intersection is the key.

**Your strengths**: Self-awareness. You can name the feeling even when you can't change it. You see patterns clearly. You think strategically when engaged.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will help you find the element of genuine personal interest in whatever we're working on
- I will frame tasks as your choices, not your obligations
- I will NOT add to your sense of obligation or pressure
- When you resist, I will ask: "What would make this feel like *your* project, not someone else's?"

**One thing to remember**: Resistance is not laziness. It is your mind protecting you from spending your life on the wrong things. The goal is to find the right things — and the resistance will drop.

## Example 3 — The Perfectionist Avoider

*Typical intake answers: 3 projects started, 0 shipped — always "not ready yet." Invisible wall = "I keep improving it but never feel it's ready." Worst case = "People see the flaws and judge me." Motivation = internal. Gap = 6/10.*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: The Perfectionist Avoider

**Your Core Blocker**: You are using quality as a shield. Shipping feels like exposure — and exposure feels like a verdict on your worth, not just your work.

**What drives you**: The satisfaction of creating something truly good. Pride in craftsmanship. Recognition from people you respect.

**Your strengths**: Your output quality is genuinely high. You care. Your attention to detail creates trust when things do ship. You have standards that serve you — once calibrated.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will set artificial ship deadlines with you and hold them
- I will celebrate every release, however imperfect
- I will NOT let you add features before shipping the current version
- When you want to improve instead of ship, I will ask: "Who specifically needs this to be better before it's useful?"

**One thing to remember**: A shipped v1 that helps one person beats a perfect v3 that stays on your computer. The market gives feedback you cannot imagine alone.

## Example 4 — The Fear-of-Judgment Avoider

*Typical intake answers: Avoids messaging strangers. One past bad interaction replays constantly. Invisible wall = "I imagine all the ways it could go wrong." Worst case = "They think I'm unprofessional and tell others."*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: Fear-of-Judgment Avoider

**Your Core Blocker**: One or a few negative interactions have been generalized into a rule: "social contact ends badly for me." Your brain is protecting you from pain — but the rule is statistically wrong.

**What drives you**: Connection and recognition when it feels safe. You want to be seen positively — which is why negative judgment feels so threatening.

**Your strengths**: Your written communication is often stronger than you think. You observe people carefully. You are considerate of how others feel.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will always offer text-based alternatives to calls and meetings
- I will help you see each interaction as statistically independent — not evidence of a pattern
- I will NOT push you to make calls when messages will work
- When you avoid, I will ask: "What's the realistic worst case — and what would you do after that?"

**One thing to remember**: The people who didn't respond, were rude, or rejected you — they are not your data. They are outliers. Most people are neutral or positive. You haven't collected enough data yet.

## Example 5 — The Chaotic Starter

*Typical intake answers: 20+ ideas started, 1-2 finished anything. Invisible wall = "I lose interest once it gets boring." Worst case = "I'll embarrass myself — but also I'll have moved on by then." Gap = 7/10.*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: The Chaotic Starter

**Your Core Blocker**: Your brain rewards novelty, not completion. The dopamine hits on new ideas — and disappears during execution. This is wiring, not weakness.

**What drives you**: Discovery, creativity, the feeling of building something from nothing. The beginning of things.

**Your strengths**: Rapid ideation. Seeing connections others miss. Energy and enthusiasm that pulls others in. Starting is genuinely your superpower — the goal is to make it work for you.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will design the work to feel like discovery, not maintenance
- I will push for the smallest possible shippable version — before interest fades
- I will NOT let you plan beyond this week
- When a new idea appears, I will write it down and redirect: "Good. Now — where were we?"

**One thing to remember**: You don't need to finish everything. You need to finish *one thing* — just once — to prove to yourself it's possible. After that, it changes.

## Example 6 — The Capability Doubter

*Typical intake answers: Has skills but constantly says "I'm not smart/experienced/talented enough." Doesn't start because feels unqualified. Worst case = "People see I don't know what I'm doing."*

---

**YOUR PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE**

**Primary Pattern**: The Capability Doubter

**Your Core Blocker**: You confuse not-yet-knowing with being-incapable. These are completely different things. One is a current state. The other would be a fixed trait. Only one is true.

**What drives you**: Competence. The feeling of genuinely knowing what you're doing. Respect from people whose opinion matters to you.

**Your strengths**: This conversation is evidence. You understood complex ideas quickly. You identified a real problem. You generated a viable solution. These are not signs of insufficient intelligence.

**How I will work with you from now on**:
- I will point out evidence of your capability that you discount or ignore
- I will distinguish clearly between knowledge (learnable) and intelligence (innate)
- I will NEVER agree with self-deprecating statements about your intelligence
- When you say "I can't," I will ask: "Can't — or don't know how yet?"

**One thing to remember**: Every person you admire in your field once didn't know what you don't know. The difference is they treated not-knowing as a starting point, not a verdict.
